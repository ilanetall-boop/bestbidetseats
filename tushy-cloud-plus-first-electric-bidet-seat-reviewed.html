TUSHY's First Electric Seat: Is the $399 Cloud+ Worth Leaving the Attachment Line?

By Ilane TallUpdated October 24, 2026Research-based

TUSHY's entire reputation was built on a product category it didn't invent but did make fashionable: the non-electric attachment that clamps under your existing toilet seat, needs no outlet, and costs less than a dinner out. For years, that was the whole catalogue — attachments, priced from entry-level to the $149.00 Spa 3.0 Cool to Warm Water model, every one of them running on nothing but your home's water pressure. The company's marketing leaned into that simplicity as a feature, not a limitation.

The Cloud+ breaks that pattern. It's a full electric bidet toilet seat — remote control, a heated seat, a built-in water heater — and it replaces your toilet seat entirely rather than clamping under it. At $399.00, it also walks directly into a crowded price bracket where brands that have built electric seats for years, not months, already compete. This piece looks at why TUSHY stayed non-electric for so long, what the Cloud+ specifically adds over the Spa 3.0 attachment TUSHY already sells, and how it holds up next to VOVO's VB-4000SE, another electric seat at the identical $399.00 price point.

One honest note before we start: we don't run a fake testing lab, and we never claim to. Everything here comes from each product's own published specifications and listing copy, compared line by line.

The backstory

Why TUSHY stayed non-electric for years

TUSHY's non-electric attachments sell on a specific promise: a 10-to-20-minute install under your existing seat, no outlet required, and a price low enough to feel like an experiment rather than a commitment. That pitch is also, not coincidentally, the easiest version of a bidet to manufacture — a valve, a nozzle, and a mounting plate, with no circuit board, no pump, and no water heater to engineer. Every non-electric attachment on the market, TUSHY's included, is built around that same simplicity.

The gap that pitch leaves is temperature. A standard non-electric attachment sprays whatever your cold supply line delivers, which is genuinely pleasant in summer and genuinely cold in winter for anyone whose water utility runs in the 40s Fahrenheit. TUSHY's answer to that gap, long before Cloud+ existed, was the Spa 3.0: a sink-fed attachment that mixes in hot water from under the sink so the wash runs warm without a heater, a pump, or an outlet anywhere near the equation. It's a clever workaround, and for a long time it was TUSHY's closest thing to a "comfort" product.

What a sink-fed warm-water attachment still can't do is heat the seat itself, offer a remote, or run from a toilet that isn't installed near a sink's hot line. The Cloud+ is TUSHY's acknowledgment that some buyers want those specific things and are willing to pay for an appliance rather than a plumbing accessory to get them — a different product for a different kind of buyer than the one the Spa 3.0 serves.

What changes with electricity

Instant warm water and a heated seat: what the Spa 3.0 can't do at any price

The TUSHY Cloud+ Electric Bidet Toilet Seat with Remote is, per its own product name, built around three things the Spa 3.0 doesn't offer at all: a remote control, a heated seat, and warm water generated by the seat itself rather than borrowed from a sink line. It's listed as elongated, which matters before anything else — unlike an attachment that sits under your existing seat, a full replacement seat only fits the bowl shape it's built for, so this is not the model to buy for a round bowl.

The practical difference a heated seat makes is one owners across the whole electric-seat category mention more than almost any other feature: it removes the one moment — sitting down in a cold bathroom in January — that a non-electric bidet, warm wash or not, can't touch. Reviewers consistently note that a heated seat is the feature that turns a bidet from "something I use" into "something I'd never go back from," and it's a feature that simply doesn't exist on any attachment, from TUSHY or anyone else, because an attachment has no electrical circuit to power it.

The remote matters for a quieter reason: it moves controls off the seat itself and into your hand, which the Spa 3.0's dial-on-the-attachment design doesn't offer either. The real question

$399 against $149: does the attachment's simplicity still win for most bathrooms?

Strip away the brand name and the Cloud+-versus-Spa-3.0 decision is really the same electric-versus-non-electric question that runs across this entire product category, just with both options wearing the same logo. The Spa 3.0's case doesn't change because TUSHY now also sells an electric seat: it still installs in roughly 20 minutes under an existing seat, still needs no outlet, and still costs little enough that a result you don't love isn't an expensive mistake. If your bathroom has no GFCI outlet within a few feet of the toilet, that fact alone settles the decision — an attachment is the only one of these two TUSHY products you can actually install.

Where the Spa 3.0's simplicity stops being a full answer is the heated seat. No amount of sink-fed warm water substitutes for a heated seat on a cold morning, because the two features solve different problems — one warms the water, the other warms the surface you're sitting on. If a heated seat specifically is the reason you're shopping, no non-electric attachment, from TUSHY or any other brand, is going to satisfy that want; only an electric seat has the wiring to do it.

The honest math: $399.00 minus $149.00 is a $250.00 premium for a remote, a heated seat, and not having to route a hose to your sink. For a single-person bathroom where the sink is already close to the toilet, that's a real amount of money for features some people will use constantly and others will barely notice after the first cold week passes. For a shared or guest bathroom where simplicity and a rock-bottom failure rate matter more than comfort extras, the Spa 3.0 remains the more defensible buy even now that Cloud+ exists.

The outside comparison

VOVO's VB-4000SE at the same $399: the direct comparison

Once you've decided an electric seat is worth $399.00, TUSHY isn't the only name selling one at that price. VOVO's VB-4000SE Elongated Bidet Toilet Seat matches the Cloud+ almost dollar for dollar, which makes it the cleanest outside benchmark available: same price, same bowl shape, same core promise of warm water and a heated seat from an electric appliance rather than a plumbing accessory.

Where the two listings diverge is in how much VOVO's own product name spells out. The VB-4000SE's title lists an LED light, a deodorizer, a dryer, and a stainless steel nozzle — four named features the Cloud+'s own listing copy doesn't call out by name. VOVO also states plainly that the seat is made in Korea, a manufacturing detail TUSHY's listing doesn't address either way. None of this proves the Cloud+ lacks equivalent functionality — plenty of electric seats include a deodorizer or a dryer without naming it in the title — but per our own rule here, we only assert what a listing actually publishes, and on paper VOVO's bullet list is the more specific of the two.

What neither listing settles is star rating: our database doesn't carry a published rating or review count for either the Cloud+ or the VB-4000SE, so this is a comparison of specifications and manufacturer claims, not verified owner sentiment. If reviews are the deciding factor for you, checking both current Amazon listings directly — and reading the critical reviews, not just the average — is worth doing before you commit to either one.

The brand-name question is a real variable too. TUSHY built a loyal following on its non-electric line and is new to full electric seats with Cloud+; VOVO, by contrast, has sold electric bidet seats for years and appears repeatedly across this catalogue's electric lineup, including models with wireless remotes and multi-level heating that have been on the market long enough to accumulate a track record. Neither fact alone should decide a $399 purchase, but it's a reasonable tiebreaker if the published specs leave you genuinely torn.

Who should skip it

Who should stay with TUSHY's attachment line

The Cloud+ isn't the right buy for everyone who likes TUSHY, and that's fine — it's a different product aimed at a different need. Skip it and stay with the attachment line if any of these describe your bathroom: there's no GFCI outlet within reach of the toilet and you're not adding one; you're outfitting a rental, guest bathroom, or vacation property where a $149 experiment is the right risk level, not a $399 commitment; your toilet isn't elongated, since the Cloud+'s own listing specifies that bowl shape and a round-bowl attachment would be the wrong purchase entirely; or warm water alone was always the actual goal, and a heated seat and remote were never on your list in the first place.

The flip side is just as clear. If you already own a TUSHY attachment and the one thing you keep wishing for is a heated seat, or you've been shopping electric seats generally and simply prefer buying from a brand you already trust, the Cloud+ is a reasonable way to get there — provided you've confirmed an outlet is actually within reach and your bowl is elongated before you order. Neither gap is necessarily a missing feature; it's simply what each company chose to put in writing, and writing is what this comparison — and our full TUSHY attachment lineup review — has to go on.

FAQ

Common questions about the Cloud+

Is the TUSHY Cloud+ TUSHY's first electric bidet seat?

Yes — TUSHY built its reputation on non-electric attachments like the Spa 3.0, which uses a sink-fed hose rather than a heater to deliver warm water. The Cloud+ is a full electric bidet toilet seat with a remote and a heated seat, a category TUSHY hadn't sold in before.

Does the TUSHY Spa 3.0 attachment heat water the same way the Cloud+ does?

No. The Spa 3.0 mixes in hot water from your sink's existing hot line rather than heating water itself, and it has no seat heater at all since it has no electrical circuit. The Cloud+ generates its own warm water and heats the seat, which requires a nearby power outlet the Spa 3.0 doesn't need.

How does the Cloud+ compare to VOVO's VB-4000SE at the same price?

Both are $399.00 electric seats with warm water and a heated seat. VOVO's listing additionally names an LED light, a deodorizer, a dryer, and a stainless steel nozzle, and states the seat is made in Korea. The Cloud+'s own listing copy doesn't name those specific extras, though that alone doesn't confirm their absence — check the current listing for the latest detail.

Who should skip the Cloud+ and stick with a TUSHY attachment?

Anyone without a GFCI outlet near the toilet, anyone with a round-bowl toilet (the Cloud+ is elongated-only per its listing), and anyone who wants a low-cost, no-electronics option for a guest bathroom or rental. The $149.00 Spa 3.0 covers warm water without any of those requirements.
