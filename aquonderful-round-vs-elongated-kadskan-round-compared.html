The math

Is tankless worth roughly fifty dollars more?

At current prices, KADSKAN's round seat costs $209.99 against $159.99 for either Aquonderful model — a $50 difference, somewhat wider than the roughly $40 gap you'll sometimes see quoted when Amazon's pricing on either listing shifts. Whichever number is live when you're reading this, the trade is the same shape: you're paying a premium in the neighborhood of $40 to $50 for a heating method the seller is willing to name outright, versus a seat that promises warm water without specifying how it stays warm.

Whether that premium is worth it comes down to one honest question about your household: does more than one person use this toilet back to back, and does anyone take a longer wash? A reservoir-style heater's limitation, when it has one, shows up at the margins — the second or third use in quick succession, or a wash that runs past the thirty-second mark most small tanks are sized for. A single person with a quick routine may never notice the difference regardless of which heating method is actually inside Aquonderful's seat. A household of three or four, especially one that hits the bathroom in the same ten-minute window every morning, is exactly the use case where a seller is willing to print the word "instant" on the box, because it's the claim that holds up under repeated back-to-back use.

If you already know your household falls in the second group, the $50 gap is a small price for not having to think about it. If you're not sure, the lower-cost move is to buy whichever Aquonderful model fits your bowl, live with it for a few weeks, and only upgrade to KADSKAN's named tankless system if you actually run into a cold second half of a wash. That costs you nothing extra if it turns out you never notice — and it's a cheaper way to find out than guessing up front.

Before any of this applies

Measure your bowl before either brand becomes relevant

Every seat in this comparison is shape-locked. Aquonderful's 102DS-W is built for round bowls; its 102S-W sibling is built for elongated bowls at 15 by 20 inches; KADSKAN's seat is built for round bowls only. None of the three is a universal fit, and a seat mounted on the wrong bowl shape will leave a visible gap at best and fail to seal properly at worst.

Measuring takes about two minutes and no tools beyond a tape measure. With the lid up, measure from the two bolt holes at the back of the bowl straight forward to the front edge of the china. A round bowl typically measures about 16.5 inches from bolts to front edge; an elongated bowl runs closer to 18.5 inches and has a visibly more oval, egg-shaped front rather than a rounded one. If you're at all unsure which you have, measure before you compare a single spec sheet further — the best heater in the world doesn't matter on the wrong bowl shape, and it rules out two of these three seats before price or features even enter the decision.

Reading the pattern

The broader lesson in a $0 gap and a $50 one

Put the two gaps next to each other and a pattern emerges that's worth carrying into any bidet seat search, not just this one. Within a single brand's lineup, a shape difference — round versus elongated — tends to cost little or nothing extra, because the shell is a small part of what you're paying for. Across brands, a named feature difference — instant heating that one seller is willing to print in a title and another isn't — tends to carry a real premium, because it's the thing the seller is confident enough to stake a claim on. Our piece on three seats sharing an identical $169.99 price point found the inverse case: brands charging the same price for genuinely different feature lists. Here, two models from one brand charge the same price for what reads as the same feature list in different words, and a third brand charges more for one feature it's willing to name outright.

That gives you a simple rule of thumb for a lineup like this: don't pay extra within a brand for bowl shape alone, and do expect to pay extra across brands for a heating or hardware claim the listing actually spells out. If a seller won't name the mechanism — tankless versus reservoir, stainless versus unspecified metal, an IPX rating versus a bare "waterproof" claim — treat the silence as information, the way we laid out in 'waterproof' vs IPX4. If a seller does name it, that's usually the one line item actually worth the price difference.

For fit-adjacent questions beyond bowl shape — a remote you can mount on the wall, or modes built for kids and for saving power — the differences tend to follow the same rule: named and specific is a real feature, unnamed is an open question. FAQ

Common questions

Do Aquonderful's round and elongated models actually differ besides bowl shape?

Based on the listing titles, not substantially. Both name a heated seat, unlimited warm water, a remote, a dryer function and a night light. The elongated listing additionally names a self-cleaning nozzle and a soft-close lid; the round listing names "Turbo Wash" instead of using that exact dryer phrasing. A word missing from a title isn't proof a feature is missing from the seat — check the full listing if a specific detail matters to you.

Is "unlimited warm water" the same thing as instant or tankless heating?

Not necessarily. "Instant" and "tankless" are the specific terms manufacturers use for a heater that warms water on demand as it flows, so it can't run low. Aquonderful's listings use "unlimited warm water" without either term, and KADSKAN's listing names "Instant Warm Water" directly. We can't confirm Aquonderful's internal heating method from the title alone, so we don't assert it's tankless or a reservoir — only that the word isn't on the label.

Is KADSKAN's roughly $50 premium over Aquonderful worth it?

It depends on how your household uses the bathroom. If more than one person washes back-to-back, or anyone runs a longer wash, a named instant heater removes a risk a reservoir-style system can carry at the margins. A single user with a quick routine may never notice a difference either way. If you're unsure, start with whichever Aquonderful model fits your bowl and upgrade only if a cold second half of a wash actually becomes a problem.

How do I know if my toilet is round or elongated?

With the lid up, measure from the two rear bolt holes straight forward to the front edge of the bowl. Round bowls measure about 16.5 inches with a rounded front; elongated bowls run closer to 18.5 inches with a more oval, egg-shaped front edge, matching the 15-by-20-inch footprint Aquonderful lists for its elongated model. Measure before comparing any other spec — it rules out two of these three seats immediately.
