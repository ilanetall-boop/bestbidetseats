Before you buy

Four things that decide whether you'll love it

01

Heater type

"Instant" (tankless) gives endless warm water; "reservoir" runs lukewarm after ~30s. For two people, instant is worth it.

02

Elongated vs round

Measure your bowl. Most modern toilets are elongated — a round seat leaves a gap. Every electric pick here fits elongated.

03

An outlet nearby

Electric seats need a GFCI outlet beside the toilet. No outlet? A non-electric option installs in 20 minutes with none.

04

Remote or panel

Side panels are cheaper but awkward; a wireless remote is the upgrade owners mention most. Worth the small premium.

FAQ

Common questions

Do I need a plumber to install one?

Almost never. Electric seats swap onto your existing bowl and plug into a nearby GFCI outlet; owners routinely report a 20–40 minute DIY install with the included T-valve. The only prerequisite for electric models is an outlet within reach.

Electric vs non-electric — which should I get?

Electric gives you warm water and a heated seat — the comfort upgrades people fall in love with — but needs an outlet. Non-electric options cost far less, need no power, and still deliver the core hygiene benefit with cold water. Have an outlet and a modest budget? Go electric.

Will it fit my toilet?

Measure your bowl shape (round vs elongated) and the mounting spread. Every electric pick here fits standard elongated one- and two-piece toilets; the LUXE and TUSHY attachments fit most existing seats. Skirted or French-curve designs are the usual exceptions — check the listing's fit notes.

Is warm water worth the extra money?

For most daily users, yes — it's the feature owners say turned a novelty into a habit. If you're only testing the idea, the $49.99 LUXE NEO 185 attachment is the low-risk way to find out before committing to an electric seat.
