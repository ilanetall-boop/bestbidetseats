The LUXE Bidet Lineup, Reviewed

By Ilane TallUpdated October 28, 2026Research-based.

LUXE Bidet's catalogue in our database is the smallest brand lineup we've reviewed: three listings, all non-electric attachments, all built around the same NEO platform. Two of them are the same model, the NEO 120, sold in two colors. The third is the step-up NEO 185, priced exactly $10.00 higher. That's a far narrower spread than the 12-to-1 range we found across Bio Bidet's four tiers in our Bio Bidet lineup review, and it makes LUXE a different, more focused kind of comparison: not "which tier fits my budget," but "does the $10 jump buy anything, and does the color choice cost anything at all."

What makes this lineup worth a closer look is the color. One of the three listings — the NEO 120 in blue — is the only attachment in our entire catalogue that isn't white. Every other bidet seat and attachment we track, electric or not, ships in white as the default and only color on record, the same pattern our broader look at color options across the catalogue documents for full seat replacements. LUXE's blue NEO 120 is the one exception among attachments specifically, which makes it worth asking directly: does LUXE charge a premium for color, the way some brands do for a non-standard finish?

One honest note before we start: we don't run a fake testing lab, and we never pretend to. Everything below comes from LUXE's own published listing details for these three specific products — names, prices, and the one documented size spec we have for each — not from owning or installing them ourselves. Where LUXE's listings don't name a spec, we say so rather than guess.

The $10 question

NEO 120 vs NEO 185: what the extra few dollars actually buys

Start with the plain numbers. The NEO 120 is $39.99 in either color. The NEO 185 is $49.99 — a $10.00 premium, or about 25% more than the entry model. That's a modest gap compared to the jumps we've documented elsewhere in the category: our KERDE lineup review covers a $20 jump between two electric seats, and our Delta Refresh Standard vs Premium review covers a $25 gap. LUXE's own step-up, by comparison, is the smallest dollar jump we've found inside a single non-electric attachment line.

Both the NEO 120 and the NEO 185 are single-nozzle attachments: one rear-wash nozzle per unit, with water pressure you adjust by hand rather than through any kind of digital control. That's the core mechanism, and on the documented specs it's identical across both models — the $10.00 step-up doesn't buy a second nozzle, a front wash, or any powered feature, because neither model has electricity or a motor to begin with. If you're coming to this lineup expecting the step-up model to add a feature the entry model lacks, the honest answer is that LUXE's own listings don't name one.

What the two models' size specs do show is a real, measurable difference. The NEO 120 in White lists its dimensions as 17 x 10 x 3 inches. The NEO 185 lists 13.5 x 7 x 3 inches — noticeably smaller in both length and width, though the same 3-inch depth. That's the one documented distinction between the two models, and it's a meaningful one if the reason you haven't installed an attachment yet is a tight gap behind your toilet bowl: a smaller footprint is easier to tuck under a seat without the attachment protruding past the back of the bowl or interfering with a tank that sits close to the seat hinges. If your bathroom has the clearance for either, the NEO 185's $10.00 premium is buying you a more compact unit, not a more capable wash.

What we can say with confidence, because it's the one fact both listings state plainly, is the price: $39.99 for either color, with the NEO 120 name and the single-nozzle, adjustable-pressure mechanism carried across both.

How it actually works

Single nozzle, adjustable pressure: the wash mechanism both models share

LUXE's own listing for the NEO 120 spells out the mechanism directly in its product title: non-electric, single nozzle, self-cleaning, adjustable water pressure, rear wash. That's a precise, useful description of what you're actually buying, and it applies across the NEO 120 and the NEO 185 alike. There's one retractable nozzle behind a guard gate, positioned for a rear wash rather than a front or feminine wash, and a dial or lever that adjusts how hard the spray runs — from a gentle rinse up to a firmer stream, limited only by your home's water pressure and the valve's own range.

"Self-cleaning" on an attachment like this doesn't mean anything electronic. It means the nozzle itself rinses briefly before it extends and after it retracts, using the same water supply that powers the wash — a simple, well-established design on non-electric bidets generally, and one that keeps the nozzle from sitting exposed to the bowl between uses. There's no pump pushing that water; your home's existing supply-line pressure does all the work, the same way it does for the wash itself.

Single nozzle and rear-wash-only is a meaningful limitation worth being upfront about, especially next to attachments elsewhere in the category that advertise two nozzles for separate front and rear washes. LUXE's NEO line, on the specs we have, keeps things to one nozzle doing one job. If a front or feminine wash function specifically matters to you, that's worth checking against other attachments before you commit to either NEO model — we're not going to claim a second nozzle exists here when LUXE's own listing describes a single one.

Getting it in

Installation: no outlet, no tool kit, and about twenty minutes

Both the NEO 120 and the NEO 185 are attachments in the strictest sense: they clamp under your existing toilet seat rather than replacing it, which is the single biggest practical difference between this lineup and any electric seat we cover. There's no wall outlet to locate, no GFCI requirement, and no electrical cord to route anywhere near the bowl — a non-electric attachment like either NEO model simply doesn't need power to do its job, because the wash pressure comes entirely from your home's existing cold water supply.

The install itself follows the same short sequence any attachment of this kind uses: shut off the water at the supply valve behind the toilet, unbolt your existing seat, lay the attachment's mounting plate across the same bolt holes, bolt the seat back down over it, connect the included T-valve to the supply line, and turn the water back on to check for leaks. None of that calls for a tool kit beyond what typically ships in the box — the mounting bolts are hand-tightened, and the T-valve connection is a compression fitting you turn by hand or with a basic wrench if one's needed for the final quarter-turn. Attachments in this category generally install in about twenty minutes, which is quick enough to do between other weekend chores and doesn't require any plumbing experience beyond locating your toilet's existing supply valve.

The one install variable that depends on which NEO model you pick is clearance. Because the NEO 185's footprint (13.5 x 7 x 3 inches) is meaningfully smaller than the NEO 120 White's (17 x 10 x 3 inches), a bathroom with limited space between the seat hinges and the tank, or a toilet whose tank sits unusually close to the bowl, may accommodate the NEO 185 more comfortably than the larger NEO 120. If your toilet has generous clearance either way, this distinction won't matter in practice — but it's the kind of measurement worth taking before you order either one, the same advice that applies to any attachment regardless of brand.

The decision

Is color worth choosing over the step-up model?

These aren't really competing questions, and that's the cleanest way to think about this lineup. Color and size are two independent decisions, not two points on the same ladder. Choosing blue over white costs you nothing — both NEO 120 colors sit at $39.99 — so there's no financial trade-off involved in picking the one non-white attachment in our catalogue. The real decision is entirely about the NEO 120 versus the NEO 185, and that comes down to one documented fact: $10.00 more buys a smaller footprint, not a different wash experience.

If your bathroom has normal clearance behind the bowl, there's little reason to pay the NEO 185's premium — the single rear-wash nozzle and the hand-adjustable pressure are identical across both models, and the blue NEO 120 lets you add a color choice the step-up model doesn't offer at all, since the NEO 185 in our catalogue is white only. Pick the NEO 120, in whichever color you prefer, and you're not giving up any documented wash capability to save $10.00.

The NEO 185 earns its premium in one specific circumstance: when the NEO 120's larger 17 x 10 x 3-inch footprint genuinely won't fit, or fits so tightly that installation becomes a fight with your tank or seat hinges. That's a real, practical reason to spend the extra $10.00, and it has nothing to do with wash performance. Outside of that specific clearance problem, the honest answer this lineup gives is that LUXE's $10 step-up is a size upgrade dressed up as a price tier — know that going in, and you'll pick the right one for your bathroom rather than assuming the pricier model must also be the better one.

FAQ

The LUXE lineup: common questions

Is the blue LUXE NEO 120 more expensive than the white one?

No. Both the blue and white NEO 120 are priced at $39.99 on their listings — identical, to the cent. Choosing the catalogue's only non-white attachment doesn't carry a color surcharge on this model.

What's the actual difference between the NEO 120 and the NEO 185?

On the documented specs, it's price and size, not wash capability. Both are single-nozzle, rear-wash, adjustable-pressure attachments. The NEO 185 costs $10.00 more and measures 13.5 x 7 x 3 inches, versus 17 x 10 x 3 inches for the NEO 120 White — a meaningfully smaller footprint, which is the one named upgrade the extra money buys.

Do either of these LUXE attachments need an electrical outlet?

No. Both the NEO 120 and the NEO 185 are non-electric attachments that run entirely on your home's existing cold water supply pressure. There's no heater, no pump and nothing to plug in, which also means there's nothing electrical that can fail.

How long does installing one of these take?

About twenty minutes for either model. You shut off the water at the supply valve, unbolt your existing seat, mount the attachment's plate over the same bolt holes, bolt the seat back down, connect the included T-valve, and check for leaks. No tool kit beyond what typically ships in the box, and no outlet to locate first.
