TOTO and KOHLER are the two names that keep coming up once you shop above the $300 mark for a bidet seat, and for good reason: TOTO invented the category in Japan in 1980, and KOHLER is the American plumbing giant that took the idea seriously enough to build a competing lineup instead of a knockoff. Between them they cover most of what a household actually wants from a premium electric seat — but they solve the same problems in different ways, and the price difference between their models is not random. It maps to specific engineering decisions.

This comparison lines up three real, currently listed models — the KOHLER PureWash E590, the KOHLER PureWash E835, and the TOTO WASHLET S2 — against the criteria that actually decide whether you'll be glad you spent the money: how the water gets warm, how the nozzle cleans, what the lid and seat feel like every single day, what installation demands of your bathroom, and what happens when a part wears out in year five.

The split that decides the price

Instant heating vs tank heating

Every electric bidet seat, TOTO or KOHLER or anything else, has to solve the same physics problem: turning cold supply-line water into something warm enough to sit through comfortably, on demand, without a delay you'd notice. There are two ways to build that, and the way a given model does it is the single biggest driver of what it costs.

Tank (reservoir) heating keeps a small tank of water hot at all times, the same principle as a mini water heater under the seat. It's cheaper to engineer, which is why it shows up on the lower end of both brands' lineups, but the tank only holds so much warm water — typically well under a minute's worth. Wash longer, or have a second person use it soon after, and the water cools toward room temperature before your eyes.

Instantaneous (tankless) heating heats water as it flows through the unit, so there is no reservoir to deplete — warm water lasts exactly as long as you want to sit there. This is the marquee engineering of the TOTO WASHLET S2, which we don't have a tank-vs-instant spec confirmed for either KOHLER model here, so if that distinction is the deciding factor for you, check the specific listing before you buy — TOTO's instant system is the one detail in this comparison we can state with full confidence, because it's named directly in the product title.

Why does this one spec justify a real price difference? Because it changes the daily experience more than any other single feature on the seat. Reviewers consistently note that the heating system — not the remote, not the color, not the brand name on the lid — is what separates a seat people use happily for years from one that quietly gets switched to the coldest setting and mostly ignored after the novelty wears off.

What actually touches you

Nozzle design and what each brand cleans with

The wand is the part of a bidet seat doing the actual work, and it's also the part owners worry about keeping clean — a nozzle that just gets rinsed with the same water it sprays isn't doing much extra. TOTO's answer on the WASHLET S2 is EWATER+: the seat generates electrolyzed water and uses it to clean the wand itself before and after every single use, so the nozzle that touches you has just been sanitized by the machine, not by a scrub brush you have to remember. It's paired with PREMIST, which pre-wets the bowl before each use so waste is less likely to stick to the porcelain in the first place — a small feature that owners report noticeably cuts down on bowl scrubbing over time.

The KOHLER PureWash E590 takes the more common approach for this price tier: a self-cleaning wand that rinses itself with fresh water before and after use, plus adjustable water pressure so you can tune the wash from gentle to firm. That's a legitimate hygiene feature and the one most buyers actually look for — self-cleaning wands are close to table stakes at this price point — but it isn't the same electrolyzed-water process TOTO markets under the EWATER+ name. If wand sanitation specifically is what you're comparing, TOTO's EWATER+ is the more thoroughly documented system of the two brands here; KOHLER's self-cleaning wand is described in its listing without that added detail, so we won't claim more for it than the listing states.

Both brands, notably, keep the actual spray itself simple and adjustable rather than gimmicky — oscillating or pulsing modes are more common on mid-tier seats chasing a longer feature list than on the models here, where the pitch is precision and hygiene rather than novelty settings.

The daily details

Seat comfort, lid close and the details you touch daily

A bidet seat isn't a novelty gadget you experience once — it's a toilet seat you sit on multiple times a day for years, and the details that seem minor in a spec sheet are the ones owners mention constantly in reviews. A heated seat is one of those: both the KOHLER PureWash line and the TOTO WASHLET S2 include seat heating, which sounds like a small thing until the first cold morning of winter, at which point it stops sounding small at all.

The lid is the other detail that quietly matters every day. The PureWash E590 is built with a quiet-close lid — the kind that lowers itself without the slam that wakes a household at 2 a.m. — plus a built-in nightlight, a genuinely underrated feature for exactly that same 2 a.m. bathroom trip. The E590's side panel keeps the controls within arm's reach without a remote to misplace, which some owners prefer specifically because there's nothing to lose in the laundry or between couch cushions.

The PureWash E835 moves control to a remote instead — the upgrade path for anyone who finds a side panel awkward to reach or would rather hand the controls to a guest without them fumbling at the seat itself. Remotes add a genuine daily-use convenience, at the small cost of one more battery-powered thing to eventually replace.

The TOTO WASHLET S2 adds warm-air drying, an adjustable feature that lets many owners cut back sharply on toilet paper use after a wash — a detail that shows up constantly in the theme that repeats across owner reviews of the S2 specifically. TOTO's SoftClose seat and lid mechanism serves the same quiet-close role as KOHLER's, engineered by the company that built the category's reputation for exactly this kind of mechanical refinement.

Before you order

Installation and whether an outlet is already there

All three of these seats are electric, which means the install question is the same regardless of brand: is there a grounded outlet, ideally GFCI-protected, within reach of your toilet? Manufacturers across the category spec a cord that reaches roughly three to four feet, so an outlet behind or beside the toilet works; one across the room by the sink usually doesn't without an unsightly cord run.

Beyond the outlet, installation follows the same pattern that applies to essentially every electric bidet seat: shut off the water at the supply valve, remove your existing seat, mount the included bracket, install the T-valve that splits your water line, slide the new seat onto the bracket until it clicks, reconnect the water and plug in. Owners across both brands describe this as a straightforward hand-tool job — a screwdriver is typically the only tool involved — completable without a plumber in well under an hour.

Fit is the other pre-purchase check. Every model in this comparison is listed as elongated — the more common bowl shape in modern American bathrooms — so measure your existing bowl before ordering if you haven't already; a round-bowl toilet with an elongated seat leaves a visible, uncomfortable gap. None of these three has a round-bowl spec confirmed in the listing data we have, so if your bathroom has a round bowl, check the current listing directly rather than assuming a variant exists.

The five-year question

Parts, warranty and what happens after five years

This is where brand actually matters more than spec sheet, and it's the real reason both TOTO and KOHLER command a premium over unbranded imports. An electric bidet seat is a small bathroom appliance with a water heater, a pump, motors and a control board — over five-plus years of daily use, something eventually needs a part: a wand, a valve, a heating element, occasionally a control board. What decides whether that's a quick repair or an expensive replacement is whether the manufacturer still stocks the part and answers the phone.

TOTO and KOHLER are both large, established plumbing manufacturers with US parts distribution and customer support lines — a structurally different position from a seat sold by a reseller with no manufacturing history behind it. That's the quiet value of paying a premium brand's price: not a longer feature list, necessarily, but the expectation that when a wand needs replacing in year six, there's a part number and someone to sell it to you.

The decision

Which one suits which bathroom

Choose the TOTO WASHLET S2 if instant heating and wand hygiene are non-negotiables — a household with two or more daily users, anyone who's been frustrated by a reservoir seat running lukewarm mid-wash, or anyone who wants the brand that set the standard the whole category still measures itself against. At $410.00 it sits between the two KOHLER models, and the instant-heat spec alone justifies that position for most buyers who've lived with a tank-heated seat before.

Choose the KOHLER PureWash E590 if you want a strong premium feature set — nightlight, adjustable pressure, self-cleaning wand, quiet-close lid — at the lowest price of the three, and you're fine with side-panel controls instead of a remote. It's the pragmatic pick for a primary bathroom where the panel sits within easy reach.

Choose the KOHLER PureWash E835 if you want KOHLER's plumbing-brand backing specifically, but the remote is the one control style you actually prefer — for a primary suite where you don't want to lean over to a side panel, or a household that wants to hand the remote to a guest without instruction. FAQ

TOTO vs KOHLER: common questions

Is TOTO WASHLET just a brand name, or a different kind of product?

Washlet is TOTO's trademark for its bidet seat line, not a separate category — it's a bidet seat, built by the company that pioneered the modern electric bidet seat in Japan. Other brands, including KOHLER, make functionally similar electric bidet seats under their own names. For the full breakdown of that naming confusion, see our bidet seat vs Washlet guide.

Do KOHLER PureWash seats heat water instantly like the TOTO WASHLET S2?

The TOTO WASHLET S2 is confirmed to use instantaneous water heating — it's named directly in the product listing. Neither KOHLER PureWash listing referenced here states a heating method explicitly, so we can't confirm whether the E590 or E835 uses instant or tank heating; check the current Amazon listing or KOHLER's own spec sheet for that model before you buy if it's a deciding factor.

Do these seats need a special outlet?

Yes — all three are electric bidet seats that need a grounded, ideally GFCI-protected outlet within reach of the seat, typically three to four feet of cord. If your bathroom doesn't have one near the toilet, our guide to what happens when the power goes out and our side-panel-vs-remote comparison below cover the practical trade-offs of going electric without easy power access.

Which is better for a guest bathroom, panel or remote control?

A side panel, like the KOHLER PureWash E590 uses, tends to be more intuitive for guests who've never used the seat before — the controls are physically attached and roughly where they'd expect. A remote, like the PureWash E835 or WASHLET S2 use, is more convenient for the household's daily users but requires a short explanation for a first-time guest.
