TOTO WASHLET S5: Is Their $580 Flagship Worth It?

At $574.90, the TOTO WASHLET S5 is the single most expensive electric bidet seat we track on Amazon. That number alone isn't the interesting part — TOTO has sold luxury seats for decades, and $580 is roughly what a serious one costs once the badge says TOTO. What's interesting is that $574.90 sits only $157.95 above TOTO's own WASHLET S2, a seat from the same company that already advertises instantaneous water heating, PREMIST bowl-wetting and EWATER+ wand cleaning — the exact three features TOTO uses to sell the S5. If the S2 already has the headline engineering, what is the extra $160 actually buying?

That's the question this guide tries to answer honestly, using only what's published: the Amazon listing text, the price, and the two Bio Bidet models that compete at lower price points — the Bliss BB1000 at $279.98 and the USPA PRO at $249.00. One honest note before we go further: we do not physically test these seats, and we do not run a testing lab. Everything below comes from the manufacturer's own listing copy and verified pricing, not a lab bench.

The engineering case

PREMIST and EWATER+, explained properly

Before comparing prices, it's worth understanding what TOTO's two signature features actually do, because both are genuine engineering rather than marketing fluff — the question is simply whether they're exclusive to the S5.

PREMIST mists the inside of the bowl with water immediately before each use. A wet ceramic surface gives waste far less to cling to, which is the same principle behind a wet-rinse on any toilet bowl — TOTO just automates it on a timer tied to the lid sensor. The practical payoff isn't dramatic; it's cumulative. Owners who have it tend to report needing the brush less often, not a bowl that never needs cleaning.

EWATER+ is the more technical of the two. The seat electrolyzes a small amount of the incoming water into a mild cleaning solution and uses it to rinse the wand before and after every use, rather than just before, after, or never, which is how most competing seats handle wand hygiene. It's a legitimately clever use of water TOTO already has on hand, and it's the detail most directly responsible for TOTO's reputation as the brand that sweats the unglamorous parts of a bidet seat.

Both features are described in the exact same language — PREMIST and EWATER+ wand cleaning — in TOTO's own listing title for the S5. The trouble, from a buyer's standpoint, is that TOTO's listing title for the S2 uses the same two names. If you're paying $160 specifically to get PREMIST and EWATER+, the S2 appears to already have them.

TOTO's own ladder

What $580 buys that the $420 S2 doesn't

Here's the honest, slightly unsatisfying answer: based on what TOTO publishes in each listing, the S5 and the S2 describe the same three headline mechanisms — instantaneous water heating, PREMIST bowl-wetting and EWATER+ wand cleaning. We don't have a TOTO spec sheet that itemizes a difference beyond that, and per our own rule we won't invent one. What we can say with confidence is the part that's visible to every shopper: the S5 is positioned as TOTO's current flagship tier and carries a list price $157.95 higher than the S2's $420.00. Flagship positioning in a toilet-seat lineup typically tracks things a listing title doesn't spell out — generation, finish options, remote design, warranty terms, or simply where a model sits in a brand's own hierarchy — and TOTO's Amazon copy doesn't break any of that out for either seat. If a specific difference matters enough to you to spend the $160, confirm it directly on the current listing before you buy; we'd rather tell you what we can't verify than make up a reason.

What that means practically: if PREMIST and EWATER+ are the reason you're shopping TOTO at all, the S2 gets you there for $420.00 — $157.95 less — with the same two features by name. The S5 earns its price if being on TOTO's current top tier matters to you for its own sake, not because the published spec sheet shows more.

Less than half the price

Bio Bidet USPA PRO — $249.00

Bio Bidet USPA PRO

Under half the S5's price, with the one omission that matters most

At $249.00 — less than half of the S5's $574.90 — the USPA PRO covers the comfort basics any electric seat should: warm water, a heated seat, a dryer and remote control. What its listing skips, and what TOTO leans on hardest to justify its own price, is instantaneous heating. A reservoir-style heater keeps a small tank of warm water ready rather than heating on demand, which is fine for a single person taking a normal-length wash but can run lukewarm if a household uses it back-to-back or someone lingers. That single omission is the clearest, most defensible reason to spend more — on either the BB1000 or a TOTO model — rather than the flashier PREMIST or EWATER+ language.

The real question

Who actually needs the flagship versus the tier just below it

The S5 makes the most sense in a short list of situations. You're renovating a primary bathroom as part of a larger project where $160 is noise against the rest of the budget. You specifically want TOTO's newest, top-shelf model rather than a step-down one, independent of whether the spec sheet shows a difference — brand hierarchy is a legitimate reason to buy something, even when it's the only reason. Or you're outfitting a home you plan to sell, where a known-name flagship fixture reads as a deliberate upgrade to a buyer rather than a budget one; our bidet seats and home resale value guide goes deeper on how buyers actually perceive this category.

The tier just below — TOTO's S2, or Bio Bidet's BB1000 if you're open to a different brand — makes more sense for most people. You get the comfort fundamentals (and, on the S2 specifically, the same named PREMIST and EWATER+ features by TOTO's own listing copy) for meaningfully less. That savings is real money you can put toward something with a verifiable payoff instead: an electrician's visit if your bathroom lacks a nearby outlet, a second seat for a two-bathroom home on a bidet budget, or simply keeping the difference.

A few practical checks apply regardless of which tier you land on. If anyone using it is on the heavier side, check the weight capacity guide before you buy rather than after. FAQ

Common questions

What's the actual difference between the TOTO WASHLET S5 and the S2?

Based on each model's own Amazon listing, both describe instantaneous water heating, PREMIST bowl-wetting and EWATER+ wand cleaning. We don't have a TOTO spec sheet that itemizes a further difference, and we won't invent one — the S5 is positioned as the current flagship and costs $157.95 more than the S2's $420.00, but the published feature names match.

Is PREMIST and EWATER+ worth paying extra for?

Both are genuine engineering rather than gimmicks — PREMIST wets the bowl so less waste sticks, and EWATER+ rinses the wand with an electrolyzed cleaning solution before and after every use. The question isn't whether they're useful; it's which TOTO model you need to buy to get them, since both the S5 and the S2 list them.

Why does the Bio Bidet Bliss BB1000 cost so much less than the TOTO S5?

At $279.98 against the S5's $574.90, the BB1000 covers the core comfort features — warm water, a heated seat, a dryer and a slow-close lid — but its listing doesn't claim PREMIST, EWATER+, or instantaneous heating the way TOTO's copy does. You're paying TOTO's premium specifically for those three named features and the brand's flagship positioning.

Is the Bio Bidet USPA PRO missing anything important at $249?

The main omission is instantaneous heating. The USPA PRO's listing describes warm water from what functions as a reservoir-style heater rather than on-demand heating, which is fine for a single user but can run lukewarm during a longer wash or back-to-back use by more than one person. Everything else — heated seat, dryer, remote — is present.
