KERDE's Two Bidet Seats: What the $20 Jump From Original to 08 Buys

By Ilane TallUpdated October 23, 2026Research-based

KERDE sells two electric bidet seats that, at a glance on a phone screen, look almost like the same listing twice. Both are white, both call themselves elongated, both promise warm water and a heated seat. One is $139.99 and controls from an armrest panel. The other is $159.99, calls itself the "08," and adds the word "Remote" to its name. Twenty dollars is a small enough gap that a lot of shoppers just grab whichever one shows up first in search and move on — which is exactly the habit that makes reading two listings from the same brand worth doing properly.

This guide compares what each KERDE listing's own words promise, section by section, and then checks where a third brand lands once you decide KERDE's own range doesn't have what you're after: GAICOM's $299.98 seat, about $50 past the original and $30 past the 08. None of these three listings currently carries a public star rating in our database — reviewCount sits at zero for all three — so you won't find a ratings comparison here. What you will find is a careful read of the feature bullets each manufacturer actually published, and an honest flag anywhere the record runs out.

One honest note before we start: we haven't sat on any of these three seats, and we don't run a fake testing lab to pretend otherwise. Everything below comes from the two KERDE listings' own specifications and GAICOM's published feature list, compared line by line against each other — not guessed at.

The short answer

If you just want the verdict

Stick with the Original if…

You're fine reaching for an armrest panel

The original gives you warm water cleaning, a heated seat with four levels, slow-close, and one-click start-stop at KERDE's lowest price. If a side-mounted panel has never bothered you on any bidet seat you've owned, there's no feature here you'd be paying extra to get. The KERDE Original ($139.99) is the $20-cheaper way in.

Step up to the 08 if…

You want a remote without giving up the panel

The 08 doesn't delete the armrest controls — its own name reads "Remote+Armrest" — it adds a handheld remote alongside them. If reaching down to the seat every time is the one thing that's bothered you about armrest-only bidets, that's a $20 fix. The KERDE 08 ($159.99) is the upgrade.

The entry point

What $139.99 buys: the original's one-click, armrest-only layout

The cheaper KERDE seat brands itself around a single idea: "One-click Intelligent Operation." In practice that means a control panel mounted on the seat's armrest, close enough to reach without standing, where a single touch starts or stops any function rather than requiring a sequence of button presses. From that panel the listing's own feature bullets describe four levels of seat heating, four levels of cleansing intensity, and water temperature adjustable from cold through warm to hot. The nozzles are stainless steel and move front-to-back during the wash rather than staying fixed in one spot.

The product title adds two more specifics worth taking at face value: a slow-close seat, and a cushion described as keeping a constant temperature "in winter" — in other words, a heated seat meant to stay comfortable rather than icy on a cold morning, which is the baseline comfort feature this whole category is built around. Nothing in the listing claims a remote control of any kind; every adjustment on the original happens at the armrest. If you've found side-panel controls annoying on a bidet seat before, that's worth weighing against the $20 you'd save — our touch-panel vs. physical side-panel controls comparison goes deeper on how armrest-style panels tend to hold up against touch panels over years of use.

The $20 upgrade

The 08's remote-and-armrest layout, and what it actually replaces

The 08's own title answers the "does it replace the original's controls" question directly: it's labeled "(Remote+Armrest)," not "(Remote)." That plus sign matters. Rather than swapping the armrest panel for a handheld remote, KERDE kept the panel and added a remote next to it — so you get a backup physical control if the remote goes missing or the batteries die, which is exactly the two-controls-at-once pattern our remote-and-side-panel-together piece examines across the handful of listings in this catalogue that build in both.

Feature-wise, the 08's bullets describe a heated seat with adjustable temperature, warm water sprays paired with a built-in air dryer for a touch-free finish, and adjustable water pressure, temperature, and nozzle position for what the listing calls a personalized clean. That overlaps heavily with the original's feature set — both are, at their core, warm-water electric seats with a heated cushion and an adjustable spray. The genuine difference the 08's own copy supports is reachability: a remote you can operate from wherever you're sitting, instead of leaning toward the seat.

One thing the 08's title doesn't repeat is the original's "slow close" claim. That's not proof the 08 lacks a quiet-closing lid — manufacturers drop phrases from a title for all sorts of reasons — but per our own rule here, if we don't have it published, we don't assert it. If a soft-close lid specifically matters to you, check the current listing images or Q&A rather than assuming the feature carried over automatically from the cheaper model.

Same brand, different words

Reading two listings from the same brand for what actually changed

Shopping within one manufacturer's lineup feels like it should be simpler than comparing across brands, but it carries its own trap: assuming a higher price automatically means more plumbing, more power, or more cleaning modes, when sometimes it just means a different control layout wearing newer marketing language. Both KERDE listings use some version of "smart" or "intelligent" in their copy — the original's "One-click Intelligent Operation," the 08's "Smart Electric Bidet Toilet Seat" branding — and in both cases the word is attached to ordinary automation (single-touch start, adjustable presets), not anything approaching a connected or app-controlled feature. Our what does "smart" actually mean on a bidet seat listing breakdown covers why that word carries less weight across this category than shoppers tend to assume.

Set side by side, the shared ground between the two KERDE seats is wider than the $20 gap suggests: warm water cleaning, a heated seat with multiple levels, stainless-steel nozzles, and an elongated fit claim in both product names. The part that's genuinely new in the 08 is the remote — everything else reads as the same core hardware described in slightly different words. TOTO's own lineup shows the opposite trap is just as common: our TOTO S2 vs KC2 comparison walks through two round-bowl Washlets about $76 apart, where the higher price does buy a genuinely different heating system — so a same-brand price gap can mean either "new control layout" or "new internal hardware," and the only way to tell which is to read the bullets rather than the price tag.

Leaving the lineup

Where GAICOM's $190 seat lands once you leave KERDE's range

If neither KERDE seat is quite right, the next step up in price is GAICOM's bidet seat at $299.98 — about $50 past the original and $30 past the 08. The most visible difference has nothing to do with electronics: GAICOM's listing specifies an Ivory finish rather than the stark white both KERDE seats use, which matters if your bathroom fixtures skew warmer. (Our bidet seats that aren't white guide covers just how rare that kind of color option is across the catalogue.)

On features, GAICOM's bullets claim instant tankless heating with four adjustable temperatures running from room temperature up to 104°F, plus four matching seat-heat settings in the same range — a more specific heating claim than either KERDE listing publishes. "Tankless" and "instant" are words worth verifying rather than taking at face value industry-wide, which is exactly the subject of our tankless instant heating vs. budget claims piece. GAICOM's listing also describes four cleaning modes — feminine wash, rear wash, soft wash, and strong wash — across three pressure levels, more named modes than either KERDE seat's bullets list, plus a self-cleaning stainless nozzle that rinses before and after use and a built-in warm air dryer with three heat levels.

What GAICOM's listing doesn't specify, at least in the title or the published bullets, is whether its controls sit on a side panel, a remote, or both — unlike both KERDE seats, which spell that out in their own names. If control layout is a deciding factor for you, that's a case where checking the current listing's images before buying beats assuming anything from this summary.

The decision

Does staying inside one brand's lineup simplify the decision at all?

Partly. If you already know you like KERDE's seat shape, its armrest panel's layout, and the general feel of its warm-water system, then moving from the original to the 08 is a low-risk $20 step toward a remote — not a gamble on an unfamiliar brand's fit, finish, or support. That's the real advantage of staying in one lineup: you're paying for one specific, legible change rather than re-evaluating everything at once.

What it doesn't do is let you stop reading. The 08's title drops the original's slow-close claim without confirming or denying a replacement feature, and both listings use "smart" language loosely enough that it shouldn't sway the decision on its own. Even within one brand, each listing has to be read on its own terms rather than assumed to inherit every feature of its cheaper sibling.

Leaving KERDE's range for GAICOM buys a more specific heating claim and more named cleaning modes, for $30 to $50 more — a real trade, not a straightforward upgrade, since you're giving up the one piece of information (control layout) that both KERDE listings happen to spell out clearly. If a published remote-or-panel answer matters to you, that pushes the decision back toward KERDE's own two options; if tankless heating and wash-mode variety matter more, GAICOM's price is the one worth paying.

FAQ

Common questions about KERDE's two seats

Does the KERDE 08 replace the original's armrest panel, or add to it?

It adds to it. The 08's own product name reads "(Remote+Armrest)," meaning the armrest control panel stays and a handheld remote is added alongside it — the original, by contrast, controls only from the armrest, with no remote at all.

Is GAICOM's "instant tankless" heating actually different from KERDE's warm water cleaning?

GAICOM's listing specifically claims instant tankless heating across four adjustable temperatures up to 104°F. Neither KERDE listing uses the word "tankless" or "instant" in its published bullets, so we can't confirm the same heating method for either KERDE seat from the available data — if that distinction matters to you, it's worth checking each listing's current Q&A before buying.

Do any of these three bidet seats have published star ratings?

No — as of this writing, all three listings show zero reviews in our database. That's why this comparison is built entirely from each listing's own specifications and product title rather than review sentiment.

Does staying with one brand across an upgrade simplify installation?

Likely yes for fit, since both KERDE seats describe themselves as elongated in their product names, but not automatically for every feature. Each listing should still be read on its own: the 08, for example, doesn't repeat the original's slow-close claim, so features don't necessarily carry over just because the brand does.
