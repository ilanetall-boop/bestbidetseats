Delta Faucet sells two electric bidet seats under the same Refresh name, with model numbers one digit apart — 893005E-WH and 893006E-WH — and a price gap of roughly $25 between them. Scroll past the model number and the two listings read almost like the same page copied twice: Elongated Electric Bidet Toilet Seat, Warm Water Bidet, Bidet Attachment for Toilet, Toilet Seat Bidet, Bidet Toilet Seat Elongated, White. The only words that change are "Standard" and "Premium," plus one more phrase we'll get to. That's not a complaint; plenty of good product lines are built exactly this way, with a base model and a step-up sharing a shell. The question worth answering before you pay the difference is simple: what does Delta's own listing text say that $25 actually buys, and does that match what $25 buys from a brand that isn't Delta?

We built this comparison from the two Delta listings themselves — reading their titles word for word against each other — and then checked the result against a third seat, from OVANX, priced within about $15 of both Delta tiers. One honest note before we start: we don't run a testing lab, and none of this comes from spending time with the seats ourselves. This is a specification and pricing comparison, built from what each brand put in its own listing and what each seat currently costs, not from owning or trying any of the three.

One name, two listings

What "Standard" and "Premium" actually mean here

Start with what Delta itself calls these two seats. Both are sold under the Refresh name, both are built for elongated bowls, both ship in white, and both are described as a "Warm Water Bidet" and a "Bidet Attachment for Toilet." The model numbers — 893005E-WH for the tier Delta calls Standard, 893006E-WH for the one it calls Premium — are consecutive, which is usually a sign that a manufacturer built one seat and then a second, feature-added version of the same shell, rather than two unrelated products that happen to share a brand.

That matters because "Standard" and "Premium" are marketing words, not spec-sheet words — the kind of label a brand can attach to almost any gap in a product line, wide or narrow. We've looked at this exact pattern before with other brands: KERDE prices its two seats about $20 apart between an Original and an 08 model, and TOTO prices its two round-bowl Washlets about $76 apart between the S2 and the KC2. In both of those cases, the honest way to find out what the higher tier actually buys is to stop reading the tier name and start reading the listing itself. Delta's $25.50 gap deserves the same treatment.

So what does Delta's own copy say Premium has that Standard doesn't? Reading the two titles word for word, Standard's full listing reads: "Delta Faucet Refresh Standard Elongated Electric Bidet Toilet Seat, Warm Water Bidet, Bidet Attachment for Toilet, Toilet Seat Bidet, Bidet Toilet Seat Elongated, White." Premium's reads identically, with two changes: "Standard" becomes "Premium," and one new phrase is inserted — "Heated Seat Bidet." Everything else, word for word, is the same string of descriptors repeated on both pages. If Delta's own listing text is the measure, the entire difference between these two seats is the words "Heated Seat Bidet."

The reality check

Does $25 buy a heated seat anywhere else in the catalogue?

Here's the comparison that makes Delta's $25.50 jump worth scrutinizing. Spend just $11 more than Delta's Standard price — $239.99 instead of $228.99 — and you land on a completely different brand, OVANX, whose own listing title for its elongated electric seat reads: "Bidet Toilet Seat Elongated Heated Electric Seats with Panel Instant Water." Unpack that string the same way we unpacked Delta's two titles, and it states three things Delta's Standard listing doesn't: the seat is heated, it's controlled with a side panel, and it heats water instantly rather than just claiming "warm water" without saying how.

Put plainly: for $11 more than Delta's cheaper tier — less than half of what Delta charges to go from Standard to Premium — OVANX's listing claims a heated seat plus an explicit instant-water claim plus a stated control type. Delta's $25.50 jump, by contrast, claims only the heated seat, with no change in control type stated and no instant-water claim added. If you value a heated seat and nothing else, Delta's Premium is the direct, same-brand way to get it. If you're shopping on price efficiency — how much documented feature you get per dollar spent above the cheapest option — the math currently favors looking outside the Refresh line entirely.

The brand question

Is the Delta name worth paying for over either tier alone?

We don't have warranty terms, parts-availability data, or review sentiment for any of these three listings in our records, so we won't manufacture a verdict on reliability or support that the data doesn't back up. What we can say honestly is narrower and still useful: Delta Faucet is a long-established plumbing and fixture brand most American shoppers already recognize from kitchen and bathroom faucets, which is a different kind of value than a feature on a spec sheet — it's the kind of thing that matters when you're buying a seat for a bathroom you don't want to think about again for years, and it's a judgment only you can make about how much that recognition is worth in dollars.

What we can measure is what you're actually trading. Choosing Delta Standard over OVANX costs you $11 less but documents fewer features. Choosing Delta Premium over OVANX costs you $14.50 more for one matching feature — the heated seat — while OVANX's own listing also claims instant water and a panel control that neither Delta listing claims. Choosing Delta Premium over Delta Standard is the cleanest trade of the three: a flat $25.50 for one feature, inside the same brand, with nothing else changing that either listing states. If the Delta name itself is worth a premium to you, pay it on top of Standard, where you know exactly what you're getting. If it isn't, the $11-above-Standard seat from OVANX currently documents more for less than Delta's own upgrade path does.

Delta Refresh Standard vs Premium: common questions

What's the actual difference between Delta's Refresh Standard and Refresh Premium?

Going by Delta's own listing titles, the only stated difference is a heated seat. Both are elongated, electric, white, and described as "Warm Water Bidet." Premium's title adds the phrase "Heated Seat Bidet"; Standard's doesn't. The price gap is $25.50 — $228.99 for Standard (893005E-WH) versus $254.49 for Premium (893006E-WH).

Does Delta's Premium tier include instant, tankless water heating?

Neither Delta listing's title uses the words "instant," "tankless," or "reservoir" — both just say "Warm Water Bidet." That means we can't confirm from the title whether the two tiers heat water the same way or differently. If instant heating matters to your decision, check the full specification table on the listing itself rather than assuming from the title.

How does OVANX's $239.99 seat compare to Delta's two tiers?

OVANX's listing title claims a heated seat, a side panel control, and instant water heating, all for $239.99 — $11 more than Delta's Standard tier and $14.50 less than Delta's Premium tier. That's three stated features for a smaller premium over Standard than Delta charges for one feature on its own upgrade path.

Is it worth paying extra for the Delta name over a lesser-known brand?

We don't have warranty, parts-availability, or review data for these specific listings to settle that objectively. Delta is a widely recognized plumbing brand, which carries its own value for buyers who want a familiar name behind a bathroom fixture. If that recognition matters to you, paying Delta's $25.50 jump to Premium is a clean, same-brand way to add a heated seat; if price-per-feature matters more, OVANX's listing currently documents more for less.
