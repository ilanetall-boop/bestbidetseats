WLJBIDET's Two Models, $40 Apart: Nightlight, Side Panel and a Curved Steel Wand

By Ilane TallUpdated November 13, 2026Research-based.

WLJBIDET sells two electric bidet seats that look almost identical in a thumbnail and sit $40 apart on the price tag. The O7L-1V lists at $149.99. The HG5-L lists at $189.99. Both are elongated-style electric seats from the same brand, both heat the seat and the water, both dry you when you're done — and if you only read the first line of either listing, you'd be forgiven for wondering what the extra $40 is actually paying for.

The honest answer is: five named upgrades, and whether they're worth $8 each depends entirely on which of them you'd actually use. We compared the published specifications and feature copy for both models line by line, reading the two listings carefully against each other, to work out exactly where the gap sits. One note up front: we don't run a testing lab and haven't used either seat ourselves; everything below comes from WLJBIDET's own published specs and pricing.

What $149.99 already buys

The shared core: both seats do the same basic job

Before getting into what separates these two, it's worth being clear about how much they have in common — because it's most of the product. Both the O7L-1V and the HG5-L are electric bidet seats built around the same four fundamentals:

  • A heated seat. Both models warm the seat itself, with the O7L-1V's listing promising "soothing warmth" and "a cozy experience even on the chilliest mornings," and the HG5-L offering multiple seat-warmth levels on top of that.
  • Warm water wash. Both heat the wash water rather than spraying straight from the cold supply line, and both let you choose between a rear (posterior) wash and a front (feminine) wash.
  • A self-cleaning nozzle. On the O7L-1V, WLJBIDET specifically describes the nozzle as activating automatically before and after each use. The HG5-L's listing doesn't repeat that exact phrase, but a self-rinsing nozzle is standard across this category of electric seat, and nothing in either listing suggests the HG5-L's wand skips it — it simply gets a different, curved tip, which we cover below.
  • Warm-air drying. Both seats include a built-in dryer meant to cut down on toilet paper use, with adjustable temperature on the drying cycle for both.

If you stopped reading right here, you'd already understand why the O7L-1V is a reasonable buy on its own. It is not a stripped-down, feature-starved version of anything — it's the complete core experience that most people are actually shopping for when they type "heated bidet seat" into a search bar. Everything from this point forward is additive, not fundamental, which is exactly why WLJBIDET can charge $40 more for it without calling the cheaper seat "basic."

What the cheaper model skips

What the O7L-1V's $149.99 leaves off the feature list

The O7L-1V's own listing names three feature groups: warm water washing (rear and feminine, plus one-touch automatic cleaning with drying), a heated seat, and warm-air drying. That's it. There's no mention of a side control panel with memory, no nightlight, no named wand material, and no soft-close lid. None of that means the seat lacks basic controls — every bidet seat needs some way to turn the wash on — it means WLJBIDET isn't naming those controls as a selling point the way it does on the HG5-L. We treat an unnamed spec the way our guide to reading stated bidet seat dimensions argues you should treat any unlisted measurement: if the listing doesn't say it, don't assume it, and check the current Amazon listing before you buy if a specific feature is the reason you're choosing one seat over the other.

What you're skipping, in other words, is almost entirely convenience layered on top of the same wash — not the wash itself. That distinction is the whole story of this comparison, and it's worth keeping in mind through the next three sections, because each of the HG5-L's named upgrades is a refinement of something the O7L-1V already does, not a new capability the cheaper seat lacks entirely.

The $40 breakdown, part 1

The HG5-L's curved stainless spray wand

What it's built from

Stainless steel, not plastic

WLJBIDET names the HG5-L's wand specifically as a "curved stainless spray wand." A metal wand is generally more durable and easier to keep genuinely clean than the plastic wands common on cheaper electric seats, since it doesn't scratch, stain, or retain odor the way porous plastic can over years of use.

What the curve is for

Angle, not just material

A curved wand extends and aims the spray along an angled path rather than straight up, which on most bidet seat designs is meant to deliver a more direct wash angle without needing to position yourself differently on the seat. WLJBIDET's listing doesn't publish the exact degree of curvature or an engineering rationale beyond naming the feature, so take the "better angle" reasoning as the general logic behind curved wands in this category, not a tested claim about this specific unit.

The $40 breakdown, part 2

Memory settings on the side panel, versus the O7L-1V's simpler controls

This is the feature WLJBIDET's own copy leans on hardest for the HG5-L: "The side panel lets you set temperature and pressure — your preferred settings are memory automatically, so you get the same comfort every time without readjusting." In plain terms, once you've dialed in the water temperature and spray pressure you like, the HG5-L's integrated side panel holds onto that setting between uses, so you're not re-selecting the same two dials every single time you sit down.

The O7L-1V doesn't advertise that memory function. Its listing describes the wash modes and the adjustable warm-water range but says nothing about the side panel retaining your last settings. That doesn't mean every wash starts from a factory default — most electric bidet seats hold the last-used setting at least until the power is cut — but WLJBIDET isn't making a memory claim for the O7L-1V the way it explicitly does for the HG5-L, and we're not going to make one on its behalf. If multiple people share the toilet with different temperature preferences, the HG5-L's advertised memory behavior is the more clearly documented answer; if you live alone and tend to leave the dial where you set it the first week, the practical difference may be small enough not to matter.

The HG5-L also packages its memory feature with a soft-close lid and seat — both lowering silently — plus an audible beep you can switch off entirely, and a soft-glow nightlight in the bowl for nighttime visits. None of those three are named on the O7L-1V's listing either. They're the kind of features that are easy to dismiss on a spec sheet and easy to miss once you've lived with them for a month, which is exactly why WLJBIDET lists all three by name on the model that costs $40 more.

The one number worth comparing directly

Temperature range: 92–102°F vs. 92–104°F

ModelWash water rangeHow it's offered
O7L-1V92°F – 102°FDescribed as adjustable across that range
HG5-L92°F – 104°FThree selectable settings: 92°F, 98°F, 104°F

Both seats start their wash water at the same 92°F floor — comfortably warm rather than lukewarm. Where they differ is the ceiling: the O7L-1V tops out at 102°F, the HG5-L at 104°F. WLJBIDET's listing for the HG5-L frames that top end as one of three selectable points — 92°F, 98°F and 104°F — rather than a fully continuous dial, so in practice you're choosing among three preset temperatures rather than fine-tuning to the degree.

A 2°F gap at the top of the range is not, by itself, a reason to spend $40 more. It's close enough that most people showering or washing at either temperature would be hard-pressed to tell the two apart blind. Where it matters more is in combination with the memory setting above: on the HG5-L, picking 104°F once means you get 104°F every time without re-selecting it, which is a bigger practical difference than the two degrees on their own would suggest.

Doing the math

Does the $40 gap track with the number of upgrades?

Line up the HG5-L's own listing and it names five things beyond the shared core wash: the curved stainless wand, the memory-enabled temperature and pressure control, the soft-close lid, the nightlight, and the switchable quiet beep. Five named upgrades for $40 works out to $8 apiece — which, read as a flat per-feature rate, looks almost suspiciously tidy. It isn't really a rate WLJBIDET is pricing feature-by-feature; it's more useful as a sanity check than an itemized bill. None of those five upgrades individually costs much to manufacture at scale — a curved metal tip, a settings-memory chip, a slower-closing hinge, an LED, a mute toggle — which is roughly consistent with a cumulative $40 premium rather than a single expensive component driving the whole gap.

That's a meaningfully different situation from a seat where one marquee feature (an instant tankless heater, say, instead of a reservoir) accounts for most of a price jump by itself. Here, you're paying for breadth of small conveniences rather than depth on any single one. If even two or three of those five — say, the remembered settings and the nightlight — are things you'd genuinely use every day, $40 for all five is a reasonable trade. If none of them move you, the O7L-1V delivers the same wash, dry and heated-seat experience those two share, and keeps the $40 in your pocket. It's also worth sizing this $40 gap against what a bigger gap buys elsewhere in the category. Our piece on why TOTO's S2 Washlet costs $25 more in round than elongated is a reminder that not every price difference between two models from the same brand maps to a pile of named features — sometimes it's one structural change. WLJBIDET's $40 gap here is the opposite case: no single structural change, just five small ones stacked up.

FAQ

O7L-1V vs HG5-L: common questions

Does the O7L-1V have a self-cleaning nozzle too?

Yes. WLJBIDET's own listing for the O7L-1V states that its self-cleaning nozzle activates automatically before and after each use. That's one of the features both models share — it's not something you lose by buying the cheaper seat.

Is the HG5-L's wand the only thing that changed?

No. The curved stainless wand is one of five named upgrades on the HG5-L — alongside memory-enabled temperature and pressure settings on the side panel, a higher top temperature (104°F vs. 102°F), a soft-close lid with a switchable beep, and a bowl nightlight. The $40 price gap spreads across all five rather than concentrating in the wand alone.

Does the HG5-L fit the same toilets as the O7L-1V?

We can't confirm that from the specs we have. WLJBIDET's listing states the O7L-1V is elongated. The HG5-L's listing we reviewed doesn't include a size line, even though "Elongated" appears in its product title — so check the current Amazon listing's fit details for your specific bowl before ordering rather than assuming from the name.

Does a higher maximum temperature matter if I usually wash at a lower setting?

Probably not much. The O7L-1V's 102°F ceiling and the HG5-L's 104°F ceiling are only 2°F apart, and the HG5-L offers it as one of three preset points (92°F, 98°F, 104°F) rather than a continuous dial. The bigger practical gap is the HG5-L's advertised memory function holding your chosen setting between uses, not the two extra degrees at the top.
