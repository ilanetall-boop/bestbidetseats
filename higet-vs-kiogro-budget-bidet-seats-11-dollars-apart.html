Shop for an electric bidet seat under $150 and you'll run into a lot of listings that look nearly interchangeable from the thumbnail: white elongated seat, a remote or a panel, a string of warm-water and heated-seat bullet points. The Higet Electric Bidet Toilet Seat and the KioGro Electric Heated Bidet Toilet Seat are a good example of exactly that pattern. Higet lists at $118.88. KioGro lists at $129.99 — $11.11 more, close enough to round to the "$11" most shoppers would eyeball at a glance. Both are elongated electric seats with warm water, a heated seat and a self-cleaning nozzle named right in the title. On price alone, there's barely a gap worth discussing.

The gap that matters is in what each listing names beyond that shared base. We read both product titles and listing copy line by line to work out exactly what $11.11 separates — not a guess about which seat "feels" nicer, since we haven't installed either one, but a direct comparison of what each manufacturer has chosen to publish. One honest note up front: we don't run a testing lab and we haven't physically tested either seat. Everything below comes from Higet's and KioGro's own published listings and current Amazon pricing.

What $118.88 already buys

The shared core: both seats cover the same basics

Before getting into the $11.11 gap, it's worth being clear about how much these two listings actually have in common — because it's most of the product. Higet's title names instant warm water, a heated seat, a warm-air dryer, a self-cleaning stainless steel nozzle, an LED night light and a soft-close lid, all on an elongated seat with a wireless remote. KioGro's title names a touch panel, warm water wash, a self-cleaning nozzle, a warm-air dryer, a deodorizer, a soft-close lid, an LED display and night light, "easy install," on an elongated seat. Lined up side by side, four fundamentals appear on both:

  • Warm water. Higet's listing specifically labels its heating system "Instant Warm Water" — water heated on demand rather than drawn from a reservoir that can run lukewarm after a short wash. KioGro's title says only "Warm Water Wash," without the word "instant" attached. That's a difference in how specific the copy is, not evidence either way about which seat uses a tank and which heats on demand — if the heating method is the deciding factor for you, that's worth confirming on the current KioGro listing before you buy, rather than assuming either way from the title alone.
  • A heated seat. Both titles name a heated seat outright, with no qualification on either side.
  • A self-cleaning nozzle. Higet calls it a "Self-Cleaning Stainless Steel Nozzle"; KioGro simply says "Self-Cleaning Nozzle" without naming the material. Both rinse the nozzle automatically rather than requiring you to clean it by hand, which is the part of the claim that matters day to day — the stainless-vs-unnamed-material distinction is a durability question, not a hygiene one.
  • A warm-air dryer and a soft-close lid. Both listings name a warm-air dryer and a soft-close lid, so neither of those is part of the $11.11 gap — they're baseline on both seats.

If you stopped reading right here, you'd already understand why the Higet is a perfectly reasonable buy on its own at $118.88. It isn't a stripped-down, feature-starved version of anything — it's the complete core experience most people are actually shopping for when they search "electric bidet seat under $150." Everything from here is additive on KioGro's side, not fundamental, which is exactly why a bidet seat $11.11 more expensive doesn't need to call the cheaper one "basic."

One feature, identical on both

The night light: no gap, no price difference

Here's a feature that's easy to assume is part of the $11.11 story and isn't: both seats include a night light. Higet's title names an "LED Night Light" directly. KioGro's title bundles it as "LED Display & Night Light" — two features named together, but the night light itself is the same basic convenience on both listings: a soft illumination in the bowl so you're not fumbling for a wall switch at 2 a.m. Nothing in either title suggests one night light is brighter, dimmer, or differently positioned than the other. If a night light is the one feature you actually care about, it's a wash between these two — you're not paying $11.11 more, or saving $11.11, to get it.

The $118.88 breakdown

What Higet's stripped-down feature list leaves off

Wash modes

Rear and feminine wash only

Higet's title names the wash in the plainest terms: warm water for rear and feminine cleaning, full stop. There's no third wash mode, no oscillating or pulsing setting, and no named pressure-level count anywhere in the title. If you want more than the two core wash positions, that's not what this listing is selling.

Controls

A wireless remote, no touch panel

Control is a wireless remote — handheld, not built into the seat. That's a real convenience over a fixed side panel you have to reach around for, but it's a different control style than KioGro's integrated touch panel, and Higet's title names no deodorizer at all.

None of that makes the Higet a lesser bidet seat than its price suggests — it makes it an honestly named one. Rear wash and feminine wash are the two wash modes that do the actual job for the overwhelming majority of bidet seat buyers; a deodorizer and a touch panel are refinements on top of that job, not replacements for it. If neither of those refinements is on your must-have list, $118.88 for warm water, a heated seat, a self-cleaning nozzle, a dryer and a remote is a complete answer, not a compromise.

KioGro's title includes the words "Easy Install." Higet's doesn't make an install claim at all — not an absence that should worry you, just a phrase one listing chose to use and the other didn't. What neither listing does, in its title, is name the actual hardware behind that claim: no T-valve, no mounting bracket, no tool list, no estimated install time in minutes. "Easy install" on its own tells you the manufacturer wants you to expect a straightforward setup; it doesn't tell you whether that means hand-tightened fittings and a bracket that clicks into place, or something that still needs a wrench and twenty minutes of patience.

That gap between the phrase and the specifics is common enough across this category that it's worth a general rule rather than a one-off guess: when a listing claims an easy install but doesn't name what's actually in the box, check the current Amazon listing's included-parts list and the Q&A section before you buy, rather than assuming either "easy" or "hard" from the marketing copy alone. Our guide on what "easy installation" actually means goes deeper on exactly this pattern — quick-release hinges and a named mounting system are a real, checkable claim; the phrase "easy install" with nothing behind it is a sales line until you verify it yourself. Neither Higet's nor KioGro's title gives you that verification, so the honest approach with both seats is the same: look at the listing's images, bullet points and buyer Q&A for the specific hardware before assuming either install will or won't go smoothly for your bathroom.

Fit and footprint

Elongated on both — but only KioGro names its exact footprint

Both titles name "Elongated," so both are made for elongated toilet bowls rather than round ones — that much you can take directly from the product names. Where they differ is in how much more detail backs that claim up. KioGro's listing gives a stated footprint of roughly 20"L x 18"W. Higet's listing, as supplied to us, carries no size or dimension line at all. That means we can tell you Higet is built for an elongated bowl, because the title says so — but we can't give you its length or width, because that figure isn't part of what we have. If exact clearance in a tight bathroom is a concern, measure your own bowl and check Higet's current listing for dimensions directly rather than assuming it matches KioGro's 20"L x 18"W footprint just because both are elongated.

The actual question

Is $11.11 worth it for a deodorizer and a touch panel?

Strip away the shared basics — warm water, heated seat, self-cleaning nozzle, warm-air dryer, soft-close lid, night light — and the entire $11.11 gap between these two listings comes down to three named items on KioGro's side: an integrated touch panel instead of a wireless remote, a deodorizer, and an LED display. That's a narrow, specific list, which makes the math easier than it is for bigger price gaps elsewhere in this category. $11.11 for three named additions works out to roughly $3.70 apiece, read as a sanity check rather than an itemized bill — none of those three individually costs much to add at scale, which is roughly consistent with a small cumulative premium rather than one expensive component driving the whole gap.

Whether that's worth it depends on which of the three you'd actually use. If odor control is something you specifically want and Higet's listing doesn't claim it at all, that alone may justify the $11.11 on its own — a deodorizer is the one addition here that isn't a refinement of something Higet already does, it's a function Higet's listing simply doesn't offer. The touch panel is more of a style preference than an upgrade in capability: a wireless remote and an integrated panel both let you control the same wash modes, just from different places, and reasonable people prefer each for different reasons. The LED display is the smallest consideration of the three, mostly a convenience layered on a night light both seats already include.

If none of the three KioGro additions move you — if a deodorizer isn't something you're shopping for and you're happy with a handheld remote — the Higet delivers the same instant warm water, heated seat, self-cleaning nozzle and dryer for $11.11 less, and keeps that money in your pocket. If even one of the three is a genuine want rather than a nice-to-have, $11.11 is a small enough gap that it's hard to call it a bad trade either way.
