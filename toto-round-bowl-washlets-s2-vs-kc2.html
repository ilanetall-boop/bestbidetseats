The context

Why round is TOTO's short list to begin with

Elongated bowls have been the standard for new American construction for decades, and round bowls have receded to older homes, small powder rooms, and a handful of budget-builder installs where a few extra inches of floor space mattered. Electronic bidet seats are precision-fit hardware — a seat shelled for the wrong bowl either leaves a visible gap at the front or doesn't seat flush at all — so manufacturers only bother tooling a round-specific shell when there's a real market behind it.

TOTO, the brand that invented the modern electronic bidet seat in Japan, clearly judged that market worth exactly two shells: the KC2 and the S2, both marked Round on their own listings. That's the entire round-bowl tier of TOTO's electronic lineup as reflected in these two listings — if you want to see where the brand goes at the opposite end, our look at the TOTO WASHLET S5 flagship covers TOTO's top-of-line elongated seat and what its own $580 price buys. For round bowls specifically, though, the KC2 and S2 are the whole conversation, and the rest of this piece is about which one — or whether a non-TOTO alternative — makes sense for yours.

One caution worth repeating: Round and elongated are not interchangeable, and a seat built for one will not sit correctly on the other. All three products in this comparison are listed as Round. If you haven't measured your own bowl yet, do that before you read another word — the rest of this comparison assumes you already know you need a round-bowl seat.

It's worth sitting with why that scarcity exists rather than just accepting it. A round-bowl shell can't simply be a shortened version of an elongated one — the mounting posts, the lid geometry, the wand's throw distance and even where the seat sensor sits all have to be re-engineered for the shorter bowl. That's real tooling cost for a shrinking slice of the market, which is exactly why most electronic bidet brands either skip round bowls entirely or cap their round lineup at one or two models, same as TOTO has done here. It also explains why the price gap between the KC2 and the S2 isn't padding — every feature difference between them maps to a difference in what had to be engineered twice, once for each bowl shape TOTO bothered to support.

The feature gap

What the S2's listing claims that the KC2's doesn't

Put the two TOTO listings side by side and the difference isn't subtle. The KC2's title is short and specific: Electronic Bidet Toilet Seat with Heated Seat and SoftClose Lid, Round. That's the whole claim — a heated seat, a lid that won't slam, round fit. Nothing about how the water is heated, nothing about self-cleaning, nothing about PREMIST.

The S2's title is longer because it's doing more work: Electronic Bidet Toilet Seat with Instantaneous Water Heating, PREMIST and EWATER+ Wand Cleaning, Round. Three named features the KC2's listing never mentions:

  • Instantaneous water heating — water is heated on demand as it flows, rather than drawn from a tank that can run through its reserve and go lukewarm. Our tankless instant heating breakdown covers how rare a genuinely tankless system still is across the catalog, and why the word "instant" on a listing is worth checking carefully rather than taking at face value.
  • PREMIST — a pre-wetting spray that wets the bowl surface before each use, on the logic that less sticks to a bowl that's already damp.
  • EWATER+ wand cleaning — the wand itself is cleaned with electrolyzed water before and after each use, rather than relying on a plain water rinse alone.

What the KC2's listing doesn't say, we won't say for it. It's entirely possible the KC2 uses a tank-style heater, a basic rinse-only wand, and no pre-wetting step — that lines up with what's absent from its own title — but absence of a claim isn't the same as a confirmed spec, and we're not going to print a negative we can't back with the listing itself. If any of that matters to you, the current Amazon listing is the place to double-check before you buy, not this page.

What survives the price cut

What the KC2 keeps despite being $112.77 less

It would be easy to read the feature list above and conclude the KC2 is simply the S2 with things removed. That undersells what's still on the KC2's own listing: a heated seat and a SoftClose lid, both explicitly named, on a WASHLET built by the brand that defined this product category. A heated seat is, by most owners' accounts across the bidet category generally, one of the two comfort features — alongside warm water — that turns a bidet seat from novelty into habit. The KC2 has one of those two, confirmed, for $339.99.

What it costs to get there, compared with the S2, is giving up the three named extras above — not the brand, not the seat heater, not the soft-close lid. For a buyer who specifically wants "a real TOTO with a heated seat" and doesn't have a strong opinion on wand self-cleaning or pre-misting, the KC2 is not a compromise version of the S2. It's a seat with a shorter, quieter feature list sold at a price that reflects exactly that — no instant heating claim, no EWATER+ claim, no PREMIST claim, and $112.77 back in your pocket.

That's a meaningfully shorter list than either TOTO's, but it covers the two features most people associate with "bidet seat" in the first place — warm water and a heated seat — for well under half the KC2's price. What it doesn't close is the rest of the gap: no instantaneous heating claim, no PREMIST, no EWATER+, and no TOTO name on the lid. It does add one thing neither TOTO listing mentions — an air dryer, which our pump and dryer noise comparison covers if sound matters to you in a shared bathroom. For a buyer testing whether a round-bowl electric seat is worth owning at all, $169.99 is a far lower-stakes way to find out than jumping straight to $339.99 or $420.00.

There's a real risk in treating "closes the gap" too literally, though. The dollar math is simple — $170.00 cheaper than the KC2, $282.77 cheaper than the S2 — but a listing's title is a marketing document, not a warranty card. Neither TOTO price includes a brand name by accident: both listings carry the weight of a company with decades in this specific category and, presumably, a parts and support channel behind it, something the budget listing's title says nothing about either way. If the seat under you fails in year three, "who answers the phone" is a real cost that a $169.99 price tag doesn't show on the listing page. That's not a reason to skip the budget option — it's a reason to treat the savings as real money with an attached unknown, not a free $170.

The decision

Is the TOTO name worth the premium over KC2's quieter list?

Strip away the brand for a second and the KC2 and the $169.99 unbranded seat make a similar basic promise: a heated seat, round fit, no instant-heating or EWATER+ claims. The KC2 still costs $170.00 more for that overlapping promise. What that premium buys is harder to put a dollar figure on than a spec sheet line — it's the TOTO name on the lid, from the company that invented this product category, plus a SoftClose lid the budget listing doesn't claim. Whether that's worth $170 to you is a brand-and-build-quality judgment the two listings alone can't settle, and we're not going to pretend a title comparison can answer it for you.

The S2-versus-KC2 question is more answerable, because the gap there is attached to three specific, named features rather than a brand alone. If instantaneous heating, PREMIST and EWATER+ wand cleaning are things you'd actually use and notice, the $112.77 gap is buying exactly those three items — not a vaguer "better" version of the same seat. If you'd be paying for them without using them, the KC2's shorter list already covers the comfort feature — a heated seat — that tends to matter most day to day, at the lower of the two TOTO prices.

Our honest read: buy the S2 if you can point to at least two of its three named extras as things you'd use weekly. Buy the KC2 if a heated seat and the TOTO name are the two things you actually came here for. And if you're not yet convinced a round-bowl electric seat is worth $340-plus at all, the $169.99 option is the cheapest way to find out before you spend TOTO money on the question.

FAQ

S2 vs KC2: common questions

Does the TOTO KC2 have instantaneous water heating?

Its Amazon listing doesn't claim it. The KC2's title names only a heated seat and a SoftClose lid; instantaneous water heating is specifically named on the S2's listing and not on the KC2's. If that distinction matters to you, confirm directly on the current listing before buying.

What do PREMIST and EWATER+ actually do on the S2?

Per the S2 Round's own listing, PREMIST wets the bowl before each use so less residue sticks, and EWATER+ cleans the wand itself with electrolyzed water before and after each use. Neither feature is named on the KC2's listing.

Is the $169.99 round electric seat a real alternative to either TOTO?

Its listing claims a heated seat, warm water and an air dryer for round bowls — the same core comfort features the KC2 covers, at roughly half the price. It doesn't claim instantaneous heating, PREMIST, EWATER+, or carry a named manufacturer brand, so it's a narrower bet on build quality and support than either TOTO.

Will the KC2 or S2 fit an elongated toilet?

No — both are listed specifically as Round, and a round-bowl seat will not fit an elongated bowl correctly. Measure your own toilet before ordering either model; neither listing offers an elongated version under these ASINs.
